# GDPR Data Protection Policy

**Policy ID:** POL-GDPR-002  
**Effective Date:** March 1, 2025  
**Review Date:** March 1, 2026  
**Owner:** Data Protection Officer (DPO)  
**Classification:** Public  

## 1. Purpose

This policy defines the organization's obligations and procedures for protecting personal data in compliance with the EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) (Regulation 2016/679). It ensures that all personal data processing activities are lawful, fair, transparent, and adequately protected.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to:
- All personal data of EU/EEA residents processed by the organization
- All departments, subsidiaries, and third-party processors
- Both automated and manual processing of personal data
- Data stored in any format: digital, paper, cloud, or on-premises

## 3. Data Protection Principles

All personal data processing must adhere to the following GDPR principles:

### 3.1 Lawfulness, Fairness, and Transparency
- A valid legal basis (consent, contract, legitimate interest, legal obligation, vital interest, or public task) must be documented for every processing activity
- Data subjects must be informed about how their data is used via a clear privacy notice

### 3.2 Purpose Limitation
- Personal data must only be collected for specified, explicit, and legitimate purposes
- Data cannot be repurposed without obtaining new consent or establishing a new legal basis

### 3.3 Data Minimization
- Only the minimum amount of personal data necessary for the stated purpose shall be collected
- Regular reviews must ensure no excessive data is being held

### 3.4 Accuracy
- Personal data must be kept accurate and up to date
- Data subjects have the right to request rectification of inaccurate data

### 3.5 Storage Limitation
- Personal data must not be kept longer than necessary for the purpose it was collected
- Retention schedules must be defined for each category of personal data
- Employee HR data: 7 years after employment ends
- Customer data: 3 years after last interaction
- Marketing consent data: Until consent is withdrawn

### 3.6 Integrity and Confidentiality
- Appropriate technical and organizational measures must protect personal data against unauthorized access, loss, or destruction
- Encryption at rest (AES-256) and in transit (TLS 1.2+) is mandatory for all personal data

## 4. Data Subject Rights

The organization must facilitate the following rights within 30 days of request:

| Right | Description | SLA |
|-------|-------------|-----|
| Access | Provide a copy of personal data held | 30 days |
| Rectification | Correct inaccurate personal data | 30 days |
| Erasure ("Right to be Forgotten") | Delete personal data when no longer needed | 30 days |
| Restriction | Limit processing while disputes are resolved | 72 hours to acknowledge |
| Portability | Provide data in machine-readable format | 30 days |
| Objection | Stop processing based on legitimate interest | 30 days |

## 5. Data Breach Response

### 5.1 Notification Requirements
- The supervisory authority (DPA) must be notified within **72 hours** of discovering a breach involving personal data
- Affected data subjects must be notified **without undue delay** if the breach poses a high risk to their rights

### 5.2 Breach Response Process
1. **Detection**: Automated monitoring systems detect anomalous data access
2. **Containment**: Immediate steps to contain the breach (revoke access, isolate systems)
3. **Assessment**: Determine scope, affected data subjects, and risk level
4. **Notification**: Notify DPA and affected individuals as required
5. **Remediation**: Implement fixes to prevent recurrence
6. **Documentation**: Maintain a breach register with full details for audit

## 6. Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA)

A DPIA is required before any processing that is likely to result in a high risk to data subjects, including:
- Large-scale processing of sensitive data (health, biometric, genetic)
- Systematic monitoring of public areas
- Automated decision-making with legal effects (profiling, scoring)
- Cross-border data transfers outside the EEA

## 7. International Data Transfers

Personal data may only be transferred outside the EEA under one of the following mechanisms:
- EU Standard Contractual Clauses (SCCs)
- Binding Corporate Rules (BCRs)
- Adequacy decision by the European Commission
- Explicit informed consent of the data subject

## 8. Third-Party Processors

- All processors must sign a Data Processing Agreement (DPA) before accessing personal data
- Processors must be assessed annually for GDPR compliance
- Sub-processor engagement requires prior written authorization
- The organization remains the data controller and is ultimately responsible for compliance

## 9. Training and Awareness

- All employees must complete GDPR awareness training within 30 days of hire
- Annual refresher training is mandatory
- Specialized training for IT staff handling personal data systems
- The DPO must be consulted on all new projects involving personal data

## 10. Penalties

Non-compliance with GDPR may result in fines of up to €20 million or 4% of annual global turnover, whichever is higher. Internal non-compliance may result in disciplinary action.
